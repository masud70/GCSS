# CS Grad *Seminar*

:::intro
CS Grad Seminar is a biweekly event held by the Graduate Computer Science Society. It is meant to provide a safe and low-stakes environment for graduate students to practice their presentation skills.
:::

:::stats
- **30 min** talk
- **10 min** audience questions
- **20 min** socialising and (hopefully) refreshments
- **Biweekly** during the term
:::

Seminars are designed around a general CS audience, so that it is understandable by all CS graduate students. They also provide everyone an opportunity to see ongoing research in the CS department outside of one's research area.

## Frequently asked questions

:::faq
### When will it start?
Hopefully, this semester! We are still trying to find a time slot that will work for most of us. Please see the links in the email we sent to all grad students.

### Why would I apply to be a speaker?
You get to present a project you were a part of to other fellow graduate students and get feedback from them. Being able to explain your research problem without too much field-specific jargon is a skill on its own. You may use these seminars as preparation before travelling to some big-name conference somewhere else.

You can totally include these seminars in your CV as an **_invited speaker_** with a link to your talk on this website. Interested speakers may fill out [this form](https://forms.gle/QLzSo6ymk1jhN4HE7).

### Why should I attend these seminars?
There are not a lot of events in the CS department where students from all labs come together. We can't keep holding these seminars if graduate students don't attend them! We need you :)

Social aspects aside, being exposed to research outside of your own field is an important part of academia. There is a nonzero chance that you will find your next research collaborator in these seminars.

### My research is not good enough :(
We don't judge you based on how complete your work is or whether it was published or not. If there is a topic/questions you have expertise in, and you'd like to share it with other graduate students, that is enough. That said, we do have a base-level expectation that your talk will be academic.

### What areas/topics are allowed?
Literally anything under the computer science umbrella, which also includes topics in neighbouring fields (like mathematics, biology, physics, etc.) as long as there is a computer science aspect to it.

### I have questions/comments!
You may use our [official email](mailto:pres@umgcss.org) or reach out to one of the [GCSS Executives](../executives/2026-2027.md).
:::
